<objective>
Close G-04-10 (major, the project's core value): flippers must answer a press or a release
within 1-2 fixed steps whatever the hold or rest length, and a released bat must always
return to rest.

Two coupled defects, both diagnosed and reproduced in planning against the real world:

1. Dead time (adapter). Planck 1.5's RevoluteJoint warm-starts its accumulated motor and
   limit impulses. Holding a bat into a limit at FLIPPER_MAX_MOTOR_TORQUE 500 saturates
   both (motor +dt*500, limit cancelling it). On a reversal the stale limit impulse is a
   reserve the new motor drive must unwind first, so the bat sits still about 15 steps.
   Planning measurements (TABLE_SPEC, real FlipperController, scratch spike):

   | Variant at each commanded direction change | release after 60-step hold | press after 240-step rest | stuck taps (holds 1-12, left bat) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | HEAD (speed change only) | 16 steps | 5 steps | 8 / 48 |
   | RevoluteJoint.enableLimit(false) then enableLimit(true) | 1 | 1 | 0 / 48 |
   | zero both internal impulse fields | 1 | 1 | 0 / 48 |
   | zero only the internal motor impulse | 16 | 5 | 8 / 48 |
   | enableMotor(false) then enableMotor(true) in the same call | 16 | 5 | 8 / 48 |

   **Chosen route: the public API.** enableLimit clears the accumulated limit impulse
   synchronously when its flag changes (planck 1.5.0 RevoluteJoint.enableLimit), and the
   motor's own first velocity iteration then cancels its stale warm start, because its
   clamp range always covers it. The suggested same-call enableMotor(false)/(true) cycle
   does nothing: Planck zeroes the motor impulse only inside initVelocityConstraints when the
   motor is disabled at step time. No internal field is touched, so no version-pinned
   field test is needed. The new latency tests are the behavioral pin; package.json already
   pins planck at exactly 1.5.0.

2. Stuck up (FSM). FlipperController's settle detector reads the limit position-correction
   drift during that dead time as "moved, then settled". It enters resting with motor 0
   while the bat is still raised. Planning measurement: a resting bat sits at most 0.036 rad
   from its rest limit, which is Planck's 2 degree angular slop. A stuck bat sits about
   0.82 rad away. A rest-limit guard with tolerance 0.1 rad and two consecutive settled
   observations gave 0 / 150 stuck taps, with or without the adapter fix.

Purpose: restore "near-instant press and release, no stuck flipper" (FLIP-01, and the
GAME-03 TILT drop that shares the release path). The user found this in UAT.
Output: the adapter reset, the FSM rest guard, an engine-agnostic restLimitAngle on
FlipperState, adapter- and keyboard-level regression tests, and a FLIP-02 rig correction.
The old FLIP-02 numbers were measured against the jammed bat.
</objective>
