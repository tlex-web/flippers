# Phase 06.3: Fix slingshot deflection on flipper-root shots - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-11-24
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Each slingshot is trimmed so that flips from mid-bat out to the tip (ball at least 0.25 m along the bat from the hinge) travel up into the playfield before they touch a sling, on both bats and at every flip timing. Today no flip from any part of the bat reaches open field first. The root strip (the first ~0.15–0.20 m of the bat, where a ball lands fresh off the inlane) stays blocked by the sling's inlane corner. That is an accepted, known limit for this phase. It is measured and reported, not gated.

Measured in discussion research (seeded probes on the real `PlanckWorld`): with the 0.5 m bat and 0.2 m ball, a root flip rises almost vertically, inside x ≈ 0.64–1.10 between y 4.75 and 3.85. To pass, it needs a sling-free column at x ∈ [0.44, 1.30] from y ≈ 3.65 to 4.95, and the whole sling body (x 0.86–1.16) sits inside that column. Taking the sling out of the column closes the inlane (move left), closes the centre lane (move right), or needs about 1 m of raise against the 0.35 m limit. No table-geometry change clears the root strip under the locked constraints.

</domain>

## Implementation Decisions

### Fix geometry
- **D-01:** Trim each slingshot by sliding its lower-right vertex up-left **along the current hypotenuse** (the kick face, from (0.86, 3.85) to (1.16, 4.75)), toward the top vertex. The top vertex (0.86, 3.85) and the lower-left vertex (0.86, `INLANE_Y` = 4.6) stay where they are. The right sling (301) follows through `mirrorPath` about `PLAYFIELD_CENTER_X` = 1.45. Measured with inert slings: corner (0.95, 4.12) clears d ≥ 0.25 cleanly on both sides, and d = 0.20 clears after a graze. The rule (1) gap rises from 0.453 to 0.632 m. The pinch proof stayed GREEN (worst ratio 1.00, live 75/75). Pick the **smallest trim** that passes the D-04 gate with live sling kicks on, rather than the deepest one.
- **D-02:** Rejected after measurement, do not re-propose:
  - Moving or re-angling the whole sling: moving right shuts the centre lane (rule (1) caps it at about +0.065 m), moving left closes the inlane, raising is capped at 0.35 m.
  - Moving the flipper hinges: the root column moves with the hinge. This closes the 0.456 m tip gap or opens a drain of about 0.82 m, and moving down breaks rule (4).
  - Changing the 22.5° rest droop: at 15°, 30° and 45° every root flip still hits the sling. It also needs `planck-world.ts` joint-limit edits and changes every shot.
- **D-03:** The root strip staying blocked is accepted for 06.3. It resembles a real table, where the sling blocks the sharpest backhand shots off the flipper base. Our short bat (2.5 ball radii against about 5.6 on a real table) makes the strip wide. Fixing it needs a bat-to-ball rescale, which is deferred to the backlog (see `<deferred>`).

### Target shot (acceptance gate)
- **D-04:** The **gate** is a seeded test, with both bats, the real `PlanckWorld` through the production fixed-step loop, and the slings **live** (kicks on). Two parts:
  - **Rest grid:** the ball rests on the drooped bat at along-bat distance d ≥ 0.25 m (d = ball centre projected onto the bat axis from the pivot), sampled from 0.25 m to the tip, e.g. {0.25, 0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45}. Flip after k ∈ {0, 2, …, 10} steps.
  - **Inlane sweep (06.1 Probe A):** the ball drops at (0.60, 4.50) with vy 1.0, mirrored for the right bat, and the bat is raised at every k in 36..110. Keep the flips where the ball is at d ≥ 0.25 at the flip step.

  Pass condition, for **every** kept flip (all of them, not a ratio): the ball centre reaches y ≤ 3.85 with vy < 0 within 60 steps, and no contact with slingshot 300/301 is drained before that. Record first contact from `drainEvents()` (elementKind/elementId).
- **D-05:** Teeth:
  - **RED control:** the current TABLE_SPEC sling, (0.86, 3.85), (0.86, 4.6), (1.16, 4.75), kept as an `OLD_LEFT_SLING` constant, must FAIL the gate. Today it clears 0% of bat-launched flips. (Corrected at phase close: 3/94 on the literal any-sling gate, as D-13 records. On the own-sling gate the pre-trim sling fails with 61 contacts, 49 knocked down.)
  - **Liveness:** a no-sling spec must pass. The inlane sweep must also yield a minimum number of kept flips per bat, so the test cannot pass blind (same idea as 06.2 WR-02). The executor measures and pins the count.
- **D-06:** Root strip (d < 0.25): print the share of flips that clear as a metric in the test output. Do **not** gate on it.
- **D-07:** If tip shots (d ≈ 0.45) still clip the opposite sling's corner after the trim, do not silently narrow the band. Raise it at the look-and-feel checkpoint. (On the current table a tip flip leaves at about 60° and clips sling 301's corner at (1.72, 4.75). The mirrored trim moves that corner to about (1.95, 4.12), which should clear it. Verify this.)

The sling would then kick into the inlane and draw its rubber and sparks on the inlane wall. ### Post-research amendments (user decisions, 2026-11-24)
The research probes (06.3-RESEARCH.md § Probe Results) showed that D-04's literal pass condition cannot be met by any trim. 30 of 94 gated cross shots (inlane d ≥ 0.35, rest d ≥ 0.42) graze the **opposite** sling's lower face, and the corner trim does not move that face. They also showed that D-07's expectation was wrong: the trimmed 301 corner does not clear the left tip flip. The user decided:
- **D-13 (amends the D-04 pass condition and D-07):** The gate is **own-sling**. For every kept flip with d ∈ [0.25, 0.45], the ball's **first** sling contact before it reaches y ≤ 3.85 with vy < 0 must not be its **own** bat's sling (bat 0 → 300, bat 1 → 301), and a flip with no sling contact must reach that line within 60 steps. All of them must pass, not a ratio. A flip whose first sling contact is the opposite sling is a cross shot, governed only by the cap below, even if it later strikes its own sling (user ruling A, 2026-11-24; 06.3-REVIEW CR-01; amended in 06.4-03).
  - Opposite-sling contacts are printed as a metric. They are also capped at the measured ceiling: at most 30 contacts, of which at most 2 are knocked back down. Exceeding the cap fails the test, so a regression cannot slip through.
  - The band's upper cap of d ≤ 0.45 is required. Beyond it the ball has rolled off the tip, and the no-sling liveness control times out.
  - The D-05 RED control (today's sling must fail) and the no-sling liveness control apply to this own-sling gate.
  - D-05's "0%" is really 3/94 on the literal gate. The RED control still fails robustly on own-sling: 61 contacts, 49 knocked down.
- **D-14 (amends D-01's "smallest trim" and D-08's face-length range):** The corner is t = 0.28 along the hypotenuse: **(0.944, 4.102)**, mirrored to (1.956, 4.102) for 301.
  - The kick face becomes 0.266 m long. The user accepts this, although it is under D-08's "roughly 0.29".
  - This leaves 0.02 of t margin before the first own-sling failure: t = 0.295 is the smallest passing trim, and t = 0.30 fails by one graze.
  - Expected results: rule (1) gap 0.632 m, sling-pinch proof worst ratio 1.000 and live 75/75 on both bats.
  - The kick-face normal and `SLING_KICK_SPEED` are unchanged, per D-08.

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- User report (2026-11-23): "When i use the flipper to shoot the ball up when the ball is located at the root of the flipper it always bounces of the triangle located above the respective flipper which makes smooth gameplay difficult."
- Real-pinball reference (target-shot research):
  - A flip from the base of the bat is a same-side backhand, not a cross. Crossing comes from tip shots.
  - The sling blocking the sharpest backhands is normal on real tables ([Pinball Primer glossary](https://pinballprimer.github.io/glossary.html)).
- Measured outcomes on the current table:
  - Root flips hit sling 300 at (0.90–1.11, 4.64–4.74), leaving at 86–95°.
  - Mid flips hit its lower-right corner, (1.16–1.18, 4.75).
  - Tip flips clip sling 301's corner, (1.72, 4.75).
  - With the slings removed, root shots reach bumpers 200/201, mid shots reach bumper 202, and tip shots reach the chute or wall.
  - Flip timing barely changes the outcome.

</specifics>
